## What to say (the pitch)

**Subject:** T-Mobile vs AT&T eSIM data you can use

> Hi [name],
>
> I published a comparison of the best eSIMs for USA travel in 2026, tested on
> T-Mobile and AT&T 5G networks. It covers Saily, Airalo and Yesim with real
> price-per-GB and coverage data.
>
> I thought it might be useful for [their specific article/audience] — happy to
> send the raw numbers if that helps.
>
> [link]

**Rules that matter:**
1. **Never lead with the link.** Lead with the data.
2. **Personalise every email.** A template gets ignored.
3. **Offer value first** — the raw test data is the asset, not the link.
4. **Stagger over 10 days.** 20 links in one day reads as a link scheme.
5. **No link exchanges, no paid links, no PBNs.** One spammy link is worse than
   none.

## Realistic expectations

**Be honest about what this can achieve.**

- 278 impressions in 90 days is a very small base. Backlinks help, but they move
  a page from position 70 to maybe position 40–50 — not to the top 10.
- **Top 10 for "best esim" requires domain authority this site does not have.**
  The incumbents are million-visitor sites with years of links.
- **The achievable target is position 30–50**, which would still triple or
  quadruple clicks from the current 2 per month.

**The honest math:** going from position 76 to position 30 might take a page from
2 clicks/month to 10–20 clicks/month. That is real progress, but it is not
$500/month. Nothing in this list gets you to $500.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Week 1:** HARO/Connectively (fastest, highest authority per hour)
2. **Week 2:** Quora + Medium (publish and rank quickly)
3. **Week 3:** Provider blogs (Airalo, Saily, Yesim)
4. **Week 4:** Travel newsletters and roundup writers

**Start with HARO.** One journalist citation is worth more than 50 forum posts,
and it takes minutes.
